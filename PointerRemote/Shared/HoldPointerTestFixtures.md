## Pointer and calibration fixtures (deterministic source-level calculations; not executed)

The attitude fixtures use a right-handed reference pose with forward/top-edge `F=(0,1,0)`, gravity down `D=(0,0,-1)`, up `U=(0,0,1)`, and right `R=F×U=(1,0,0)`. Extracted wire coordinates are `x=420·atan2(P·R,P·F)` and `y=-420·atan2(P·U,hypot(P·F,P·R))`. Native row-major `CMAttitude.rotationMatrix` values are not assigned a direction in advance: the resolver compares both matrix directions against reference gravity and latches one for the stream.

| Fixture | Input / calculation | Expected invariant |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Yaw sign | pointing direction `P=(sin 0.2, cos 0.2, 0)` | yaw `+0.2`, wire x `+84`; aiming right moves screen right. `P=(-sin 0.2,cos 0.2,0)` produces `-84`. |
| Pitch sign | `P=(0,cos 0.1,sin 0.1)` | pitch `+0.1`, wire y `-42`; aiming upward moves screen up. Downward aim produces `+42`. |
| Pure roll unchanged | keep transformed top-edge direction `P=F` while rotating device X/Z axes by any roll around +device Y | yaw/pitch and x/y remain exactly zero because extraction uses the pointing direction, not device roll or two raw rates. |
| Combined roll 90° then world yaw/pitch | roll device 90° around unchanged `F`, then aim top edge at `P=(sin 0.2·cos 0.1, cos 0.2·cos 0.1, sin 0.1)` | extractor still yields yaw `+0.2`, pitch `+0.1`, wire `(84,-42)`; screen axes do not rotate with the handset. |
| Gravity-level basis | reset with `F=(0,1,0)`, measured `D=(0.1,0,-0.995)` normalized | projected down is perpendicular to F; `U=-normalize(project(D))`, `R=normalize(F×U)` are unit and mutually orthogonal. Initial angles are zero without assuming the phone is itself level. |

### Convention algebra

Let `R` be the physical device-to-reference rotation, `d=(0,0,−1)`, and therefore `gDevice=Rᵀd`. If Core Motion supplies native `M=R`, then `M·gDevice=d`; if it supplies `M=Rᵀ`, then `Mᵀ·gDevice=d`. Thus the selected candidate yields the same physical transform `R` under either native convention. The wrong candidate is generally `RᵀRᵀd`. For `R=I`, and for any pure vertical yaw where both `R·d=d` and `Rᵀ·d=d`, both candidates equal `d`, proving those poses cannot identify direction and must remain ambiguous.

For a quaternion `q`, negating all four components leaves `q·v·q⁻¹` unchanged; the two comparator errors are consequently unchanged for `q` and `−q`. After direction latches, a roll composition `R′=R·S` about device +Y has `S·eY=eY`, so `R′·eY=R·eY`: the forward extractor is roll invariant. For a rolled phone whose top edge is `P=(sin(yaw)cos(pitch), cos(yaw)cos(pitch), sin(pitch))`, the basis dot products remain `(sin(yaw)cos(pitch), cos(yaw)cos(pitch), sin(pitch))`, yielding exactly `(yaw,−pitch)` on the wire under either native convention. Finally, if the latched transform later gives `||T·gDevice−d||>0.12`, the sample is rejected even if the opposite candidate is closer; this is a physical-consistency failure, not permission to flip the stream latch.
